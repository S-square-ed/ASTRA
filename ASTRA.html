    Checked within x = 23·h1,2 (at least 300 m) around the site, in 12 sectors of 30°: 0 when the sea covers at least the threshold of one sector, I when rivers, canals and lakes cover at least the threshold of the circle, III when every sector lies for at least 100 % minus the threshold in a big-city centre, II elsewhere. Look at the aerial photo, check doubtful areas with the 10 % circle, and set the category by hand if needed.

    Intelligent detection reads the land cover of the whole circle from OpenStreetMap (residential, commercial and industrial areas, forest, farmland, meadows, parks, water, beaches) and classifies every 30° sector: sea → 0, water or bare flat land → I, open land and land that is not mapped → II, built-up and forest → III. The smoothest class covering at least the threshold of a sector sets that sector; the site takes the smoothest sector. Land that is not mapped counts as open land, which is on the safe side.

    The master analysis reads the terrain profile along the 12 wind directions of the sectors (0°, 30° … 330°) and along every direction you add, each from 3 km upwind to 1,5 km downwind. For each profile it finds the hill, ridge, cliff or escarpment the site stands on, as in the analysis above, and works out co(z) by EN 1991-1-4 annex A.3 at the reference height z. The profiles are drawn below each other on the same scale. Click a direction to open it in the analysis above; Use per direction for qp gives every sector of the peak pressure the co of its own wind direction.

    For a site on a hill, ridge, cliff or escarpment, EN 1991-1-4 annex A.3 gives co from the shape of the terrain along the wind. The terrain profile is read along the wind direction, 3 km upwind and 1,5 km downwind (DHM Vlaanderen II or Relief Wallonie, else Copernicus DEM). The crest is the highest point from 1,5 km upwind to 1 km downwind; the base level is the median of the profile upwind; H is the crest above that level; the foot of each slope is where the terrain is back within 10 % of H. The values can be typed in. Φ = H/Lu below 0,05: no orography effect; otherwise co = 1 + 2·s·Φ (Φ < 0,3) or 1 + 0,6·s, with s from the position of the site. Turn the wind to check other directions.
